# BOWG gap review — outside read, 2026-09-27

Scope: read-only review of docs + `phase1/*`, `phase0/fetch_obbi_metar.py`, plus three cheap checks I ran myself (NOAA ISD OBBI 2022–23 METARs; a 276-step ARCO sample of the OBBI cell for JJA 2023; the api's nearest-cell logic against real Bahrain coordinates). Egress here blocks Iowa Mesonet, Open-Meteo, Ogimet, OpenAQ and most journals, so anything about those is UNVERIFIED unless said otherwise.

## Top 5 gaps (ranked) + recommended fix

1. **The correction does not fire for OBBI, Manama or Riffa.** VERIFIED. The model is trained on cell 26.00N/50.50E and only fires there. But nearest-cell selection sends OBBI (26.271, 50.634) to 26.25/50.75, and Manama/Riffa to 26.25/50.50 — all `corrected_C: null`. Only Sakhir/OBBS-area queries get corrected. The JSON says "validated at OBBI" about a cell that does not contain OBBI. *Fix:* define the domain as "Bahrain" (a lat/lon box or the 4–6 cells over the island), not one cell centre; either train one offset set per cell (all are 2–20% land, so a shared offset is defensible as a first pass — state it), or snap any in-box query to the calibrated cell and say so in the response.

2. **A summer-only model is served year-round with no month guard.** VERIFIED (`api.py` has no month check; JSON trained/validated JJA only). A January 10 UTC query gets +6.3 °C added. The sea-smear bias is strongly seasonal (land–sea contrast is smallest in winter; UNVERIFIED magnitude for Bahrain, but the physics is not in doubt). *Fix now:* refuse or flag correction outside JJA. *Fix properly:* hour × month offsets (24×12) trained on 2010–2024 — Mesonet/ISD have decades, ARCO has them all; the cost is one longer cached pull.

3. **A single ±1.44 °C is not honest; the error is heteroscedastic and extremes are systematically under-corrected.** VERIFIED on my 2023 sample: per-hour corrected RMSE runs 1.0 °C (03 UTC) to 2.2 °C (10 UTC); on the hot tail (obs ≥ 40 °C, n=33) the corrected value is biased −1.66 °C with RMSE 2.5 °C. Cause: an additive offset cannot restore variance — `sd_corr == sd_era5` (1.59) vs `sd_obs` 2.43 at 10 UTC. The 2023-07-19 demo (38.9 vs 44) is not an anecdote, it is the model's structure. *Fix:* store and serve per-hour (later per-hour×month) RMSE, and report the hot-tail bias in the honesty note. Then move to a method that restores variance (see §2).

4. **The strategic comparison is against a straw man.** UNVERIFIED but the docs support it: "more accurate than uncorrected global apps" is measured against raw 0.25° ERA5, which no consumer app serves. Open-Meteo's own archive already read 42.6 °C where ARCO read 35.0 (LOG 2026-06-23) — i.e. the ERA5-Land-based product a normal user gets may already beat BOWG's corrected 38.9 on that day. Until BOWG's corrected series is benchmarked against Open-Meteo's default archive/forecast at OBBI over a full season, the moat claim is unproven. *Fix:* one experiment, ~30 min: Open-Meteo archive (best_match and `era5_land`) at OBBI, JJA 2023, RMSE vs METAR, side-by-side with BOWG. If BOWG loses, the correction must be re-based onto ERA5-Land (Phase 0 already found it the best free land baseline) — that is fine, and it is a better product.

5. **"One real external user" has no plausible candidate on the current roadmap.** A per-point API serving reanalysis with a 6-day (ERA5T) to 3-month lag serves neither real-time users (labour-safety, outdoor sport — they need today/tomorrow) nor research users (they want the whole corrected hourly series as a file with a DOI, not 4 MB-per-request point lookups). *Fix:* reorder Phase 2. Ship the artefact a researcher can cite: a corrected hourly OBBI-cell CSV/Parquet 2000–present with per-hour uncertainty, on Zenodo with a DOI, regenerated by one script. That is zero-ops, "untouched for 2 years" safe, and the most credible SCE calling card. Keep `/point` as the demo, not the product.

## 1. Methodology gaps (detail)

- **Cell choice / representativeness.** VERIFIED: land fraction at 26.00/50.50 is 0.18; OBBI's actual cell 26.25/50.75 is 0.11; Manama's 26.25/50.50 is 0.11. The trained cell is the most-land Bahrain cell, so the choice is defensible, but it is undocumented and the domain check contradicts the labelling. OBBI is a coastal, 2 m elevation, sea-breeze site; the interior (Sakhir, where the correction *does* fire) is hotter and drier by day and cooler at night — so the correction is most wrong exactly where it is applied. Unquantified (no second station, see §3).
- **Training/test design.** One train year, one test year, one season: no uncertainty on the 1.44 itself. The E08 (CDS, 1.20) vs Phase 1 (ARCO, 1.44) discrepancy is 20% on the headline number and is hand-waved as "source + snapping"; ARCO and CDS agree to 0.04 °C on the peak, so it is almost certainly the pairing, not the data. Do leave-one-year-out over ≥10 years and report mean ± spread; that also gives a trend check (is the bias stable, or drifting with urbanisation around Muharraq?).
- **Metrics.** Report bias, MAE, RMSE, per-hour RMSE, and a hot-tail metric (bias on obs ≥ 40 °C, or the 95th-percentile error). The additive model's bias is ~0 by construction on the mean, which hides the tail problem.
- **METAR quality.** VERIFIED from NOAA ISD 411500 (JJA 2022–23): 8,724 METARs, 99.4% of hours covered, temperature 100% whole-degree, dewpoint 99.5% whole-degree, obs at :00 (5,800) and :30 (4,350), essentially no other minutes. Whole-degree rounding is a ±0.5 °C uniform error → ~0.29 °C RMSE floor (in quadrature: 1.44 → ~1.41), so it is minor but worth stating. ISD also carries 1,436 FM-12 SYNOPs with tenths precision (91%) — a free higher-precision series at 3-hourly cadence, useful as a cross-check on the METAR rounding. The 30-min snap tolerance with :30 obs present means the :30 ob wins whenever the :00 is missing; fine, but note it.
- **Code review of `train_correction.py` / `api.py`.** Sign convention is consistent (bias = era5 − obs; corrected = raw − offset; hour 10 UTC offset −6.29 → +6.29 °C). Timezones are consistently UTC; offsets keyed by UTC hour; Bahrain has no DST — fine. `lon % 360` and the wrapped longitude check are correct. `to_hourly` uses `reindex(method="nearest")`, which raises on a duplicate timestamp index (a METAR + SPECI at the same minute) — it has not happened yet, so it is latent; `drop_duplicates` before reindex. Pairing uses instantaneous ERA5 vs a METAR that is itself a ~10-min-old spot reading — fine. No leakage found. `@app.on_event("startup")` is deprecated in FastAPI (use lifespan) — cosmetic. Freshness/NaN handling was fixed today in 608f91d; not re-reviewed.
- **Per-request cost.** VERIFIED: the store is chunked (1, 721, 1440), so every `/point` call pulls one full global slice from GCS (~4 MB before compression). Fine for a demo; not for public traffic. A mirrored Gulf subset (already an accepted DECISION, never done) fixes this and the ARCO-dependency risk at once.

## 2. Better approaches that stay minimal

Ranked by payoff/effort, all numpy-only:

1. **Hour × month offsets, multi-year.** Same code, more data. Fixes gap 2 and gives leave-one-year-out CIs. Effort: one cached pull (~2 h of ARCO reads at chunk-1 speed, or use CDS `reanalysis-era5-single-levels-timeseries`, already in the stack via `cdsapi`).
2. **Add the neighbouring land cell as a predictor.** My in-sample test (indicative only, n≈45/hour) with `T_obs ~ a + b·T_sea + c·T_land(26.0/50.0, lsm 0.60)`: 13 UTC RMSE 1.82 → 1.33, 10 UTC 2.18 → 1.91. E09 concluded "the residual is external" but only tried same-cell fields; the diurnal amplitude ERA5 lacks over the sea cell exists one cell west. This is physically motivated (the island's air mass is a sea/land mix) and directly attacks the variance problem. Validate out-of-sample before believing it. Skin temperature and land-sea mask are in ARCO if you want a weighted land/sea blend instead.
3. **Quantile mapping per hour (or per hour×season).** Standard for reanalysis-to-station correction (Cannon et al. 2015 QDM; Maraun 2016 review; recent ERA5-specific: Rozante et al. 2025 IJC — QM beats linear scaling in coastal clusters; a 2025 ACP paper does bivariate QM of ERA5 T and RH, arguing univariate correction breaks the T–RH dependence). Empirical QM is ~20 lines of numpy. It restores the observed distribution's tails, which the additive model cannot. Risk: with one station and 2,200 samples/hour it is fine; do not extrapolate beyond the training range.
4. **Re-base on ERA5-Land (0.1°).** Not in ARCO; via `cdsapi` (in the stack). Phase 0 found it recovers +2.4 °C for free. Correction on top of ERA5-Land starts from a smaller, less sea-driven bias. Only worth it if gap 4's benchmark says so.
5. Skip for now: MOS with many predictors, ML, dynamical downscaling.

## 5. E13 — dewpoint / heat index design

What I measured (VERIFIED, JJA 2023 sample): ERA5 Td at the cell is **too humid by +1.8 (03 UTC) to +6.6 °C (13 UTC)**, RMSE 5.8 °C. Raw RH 62–71% vs observed 36–55%. Crucially, correcting T alone and leaving ERA5 Td gives midday RH ~49–54% vs observed ~36% — a corrected-T heat index would still overstate midday humid heat. Td must be corrected with T.

Design:
- **Predictand:** correct vapour pressure (or specific humidity), not Td or RH directly; derive Td/RH after. Vapour pressure is the conserved quantity, keeps Td ≤ T if you enforce it, and avoids RH's dependence on the T correction.
- **Model:** hour × month offsets (or QM) on vapour pressure, trained/validated on the same years as T; report joint metrics: Td RMSE, RH RMSE, and HI/WBGT RMSE computed from (T_obs, Td_obs) vs (T_corr, Td_corr). Evaluate the *derived index*, not just Td — errors compound.
- **Pitfalls:** METAR Td is whole-degree (99.5%), so RH at 40 °C has ~±3 pt rounding noise; Bahrain's humidity is bimodal (shamal NW dry vs sea-breeze/evening humid maxima — cf. the 2024 Comms Earth & Env paper on post-sunset humid-heat maxima on the southern Gulf), so an hour-of-day mean may straddle two regimes; check residuals by ERA5 wind direction before adding it as a predictor. Sea-smear bias on humidity is larger and more regime-dependent than on T — expect a smaller RMSE cut.
- **Index choice:** Rothfusz/NWS HI is a regression fitted for RH ≥ ~13% and T ≥ 80 °F with the low-RH adjustment; at Td 30 °C it extrapolates off-chart. For the consumer face use HI (people know it) but cap/flag extrapolation. For the labour-safety story compute WBGT via Liljegren (needs solar radiation and 10 m wind — both in ARCO) and note Bahrain's ban is time-based, not WBGT-based. Do not ship a "feels like" from sea-smeared Td; Phase 0 already showed it triples apparent humidity.
